# Part 5 – Final Reflection
Write **200–300 words** describing your learning experience during this lab.
Consider:
* What surprised you about computer hardware?
* What components were new to you?
* What challenges did you face?
* What did you learn about embedded systems?

Before this lab, I thought of a computer mainly as a single “box,” so separating hardware from software in practice was eye-opening. What surprised me most was how modular a desktop still is—CPU, RAM, storage, and PSU are distinct parts—while the Raspberry Pi feels like the opposite idea taken to an extreme, with almost everything soldered for size and cost.

Several components were new in detail rather than in name. I had heard of VRMs and chipsets but had not traced power from the PSU to the CPU socket. GPIO on the Pi was also new as a concept: pins that are not fixed to one job but can be programmed for LEDs, sensors, or communication protocols.

Challenges included working safely (power off, discharge static) and keeping track of screws and cables during disassembly. Labelling photos and matching each part to its function took longer than expected because many chips look similar without reading silkscreen text.

Embedded systems, I learned, trade flexibility for integration. A Pi is not meant to be upgraded like a gaming PC; instead it is a complete platform for learning Linux, networking, and physical computing. Comparing the tower PC and the Pi helped me see that “computer” spans a huge range—from expandable workstations to credit-card-sized controllers—while the same ideas (fetch, decode, execute, store) apply at every scale.
